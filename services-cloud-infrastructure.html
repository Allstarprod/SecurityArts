What the engagement covers

IAM & identity

Over-privilege, role assumption, and escalation paths across your identity graph — where most cloud breaches actually live.

Network & segmentation

Exposure, lateral movement, and blast-radius review across VPCs, peering, and service boundaries.

Infrastructure as code

Terraform, CloudFormation, and pipeline config reviewed before it ships insecure defaults to prod.

Data & secrets

Storage exposure, key management, and secret sprawl reviewed against real access paths.

How the engagement runs

  1. Scope to an outcome. Agree the crown jewels and the blast radius that matters — a named goal, not a bundle of hours.
  2. Attack the paths. Chain identity and misconfiguration the way an adversary would, with the path written down.
  3. Seal. A signed, dated record: what was reviewed, what was proven safe, what is still open.
  4. Re-test. Fix the findings and we re-issue the seal — proof the fix held.

Questions

Do you cover multi-cloud?

Yes — AWS, GCP and Azure. We review each provider against its own attack surface and the CIS Benchmark for that platform, and we look at the seams between them where identity and trust cross boundaries.

Do you touch production?

Only with explicit scope and guardrails. Most of the review is read-only against configuration and infrastructure-as-code; any active testing in production is agreed and bounded up front.

How does this help a SOC 2 or ISO audit?

The findings map to CIS Benchmarks, NIST CSF and ISO 27001 controls, and the sealed record gives you evidence of what was reviewed and remediated that an auditor can accept.
