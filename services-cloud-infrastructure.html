03 Method

How the engagement runs

  1. Scope to an outcome

    We agree the crown jewels and the blast radius that matters: a named goal, not a bundle of hours.

  2. Attack the paths

    We chain identity and misconfiguration the way an adversary would, with the path written down.

  3. Seal the report

    A signed, dated report: what we reviewed, what held up, and what is still open. Sealed, so anyone you share it with can check it hasn't changed.

  4. Retest

    Fix the findings and we test them again. The retest gets its own sealed report, so you can show the fix was checked, and when.

05 The seal

The report is the deliverable

We seal the report with the studio's SecurityArts account. Anyone you share it with can check that it's the exact file we sealed, and when we sealed it.

What the seal shows

  • Which account sealed the report: the studio's.
  • When it was sealed, as recorded by SecurityArts.
  • That the file hasn't changed since. Change one character and it stops matching.

What it can't show

  • That your systems are secure. It covers the report, not the systems it describes.
  • Anything outside the scope we agreed, or anything found after the seal date.
  • An audit opinion or a certification.
  • A copy that has been edited, re-saved, re-exported or screenshotted. Only the exact file matches.

06 Questions

Questions

Do you cover multi-cloud?

Yes: AWS, GCP and Azure. We review each provider against its own attack surface and its CIS Benchmark, and we look at the seams between them, where identity and trust cross boundaries.

Do you touch production?

Only with explicit scope and guardrails. Most of the review is read-only against configuration and infrastructure-as-code; any active testing in production is agreed and bounded up front.

How does this help a SOC 2 or ISO 27001 audit?

The findings map to CIS Benchmarks, NIST CSF and ISO 27001 controls, and the sealed report is evidence of what was reviewed and fixed that you can hand your auditor. Whether it is enough is the auditor's call; a SOC 2 report or an ISO 27001 certificate comes from them, not from us.
