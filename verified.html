02 The honest part

What a seal is, and what it isn't

A seal is a receipt, not a judgement. Here is exactly what one can and can't tell a stranger.

What does a seal show?

  • Which SecurityArts account sealed this exact file.
  • When it was sealed, at a time recorded and signed by SecurityArts.
  • That the file hasn't changed by a single byte since. Anyone can check that.

What doesn't a seal show?

  • Who made the art, or whether a person or an AI made it. It isn't an AI detector, and it never guesses.
  • Whether the work is original, or who owns the copyright.
  • Anything about a copy that has been re-saved, resized, re-encoded or screenshotted. Those are different bytes.
  • Whether the art is any good. It records which account sealed what, and when.

03 How sealing works

Three steps, one receipt

  1. Upload the finished file

    Drop in the finished piece. We read the exact bytes and don't alter them.

  2. We fingerprint and sign it

    A SHA-256 fingerprint of the file, signed with the SecurityArts ECDSA P-256 key along with the time of sealing. Change one pixel and it stops matching.

  3. Share the certificate

    A public page with the algorithm, fingerprint, signature and time. Anyone can check a file against it.

04 Why seal

When someone says it's AI

Getting wrongly accused of using AI is exhausting, and arguing in the replies rarely wins. A seal won't settle that argument on its own. Nothing honest can show how a piece was made.

What a seal gives you is a fixed point a stranger can check without taking your word for it: your account had this exact file at this recorded time, and it hasn't changed since.

Keep your layered source files and early versions too. Together they tell a fuller story than a seal can alone.

And do it before anyone asks. Seal each finished piece as you post it, and the link is there when you need it. Read the guide to answering an AI accusation.

06 Questions

Before you seal

Will a seal stop people saying my work is AI?

Not on its own, and we won't pretend it will. A seal can't show whether a person or an AI made something.

It shows that your account had this exact file at a recorded time and that it hasn't changed since. Alongside your source files and early versions, that's a public, dated point a stranger can check for themselves.

Does the certificate say who made the art?

It names the account that sealed the file. The credit line is whatever that account typed in, and SecurityArts doesn't check it. That's why a seal says which account sealed a file, not who made it.

What if I re-save, resize or crop the file?

Then it's a different file with different bytes, and it won't match the seal. The original still matches its certificate, so keep it safe. If you seal the new version, it gets a certificate of its own.

Who can see what I seal?

Everyone. Sealed work is public: the image, title, credit, medium, the sealing account's name and handle, the fingerprint and the time of sealing appear on the wall and on the certificate.

Only seal work you have the right to share, and credit it honestly.
