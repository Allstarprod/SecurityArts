03 Method

How the engagement runs

  1. Scope to an outcome

    We agree what "secure enough" means for this engagement: a named goal, not a fixed bundle of hours.

  2. Attack

    Manual, adversary-style testing, with the path written down as we go. Reproducible, not a scanner dump.

  3. Seal the report

    A signed, dated report: what we tested, what held up, and what is still open. Sealed, so anyone you share it with can check it hasn't changed.

  4. Retest

    Fix the findings and we test them again. The retest gets its own sealed report, so you can show the fix was checked, and when.

05 The seal

The report is the deliverable

We seal the report with the studio's SecurityArts account. Anyone you share it with can check that it's the exact file we sealed, and when we sealed it.

What the seal shows

  • Which account sealed the report: the studio's.
  • When it was sealed, as recorded by SecurityArts.
  • That the file hasn't changed since. Change one character and it stops matching.

What it can't show

  • That your systems are secure. It covers the report, not the systems it describes.
  • Anything outside the scope we agreed, or anything found after the seal date.
  • An audit opinion or a certification.
  • A copy that has been edited, re-saved, re-exported or screenshotted. Only the exact file matches.

06 Questions

Questions

How is a SecurityArts pentest different from a commodity pentest?

We attack the way a real adversary would, goal-first rather than a checklist sweep, and scope every engagement to a named outcome. Instead of a PDF you file away, you get a sealed report. A retest gets its own sealed report, so you can show when a fix was checked again.

Will this satisfy a SOC 2 or ISO 27001 audit?

That is your auditor's call, and we won't promise it. Our testing maps to the frameworks auditors already use (OWASP, MITRE ATT&CK, NIST CSF and CIS Benchmarks) and to SOC 2 and ISO 27001 controls, and the sealed report is evidence you can hand an auditor or an enterprise customer.

Mapping isn't certification: a SOC 2 report or an ISO 27001 certificate comes from an independent auditor.

What is the seal?

Every engagement ends in a signed, dated report of what we tested, what held up and what is still open, sealed with the studio's SecurityArts account. The seal shows that this exact file was sealed by that account at the time SecurityArts recorded, and that it hasn't changed since.

It doesn't show that your systems are secure, and an edited or re-saved copy won't match. A retest ends in a new sealed report beside the first.
