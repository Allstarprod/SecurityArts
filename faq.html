02 The seal

The seal

What is a SecurityArts seal?

A seal is a signed, public certificate for one file. SecurityArts computes a SHA-256 fingerprint of the exact image bytes and signs it with an ECDSA P-256 key. The result is a public record anyone can check: the algorithm, the fingerprint, the signature, the signer and the time.

Is SecurityArts an AI detector?

No. AI detectors estimate a probability and are often wrong. A seal never guesses. It records that a specific account sealed a specific file at a time SecurityArts recorded, and shows that the bytes haven't changed since. It's a receipt, not a verdict.

What does a seal actually show?

That a specific file was sealed by a SecurityArts account at a recorded time, and that the exact bytes haven't changed since. It doesn't prove originality or settle copyright. It gives you a checkable point in time you can point to.

Does a seal prove a person made the art?

No. A seal shows that a specific account gave SecurityArts these exact bytes at a recorded time, and that SecurityArts signed the SHA-256 of those bytes with its key. It can't see how the file was made, so it doesn't prove the work was made by a person or that it's original. Anyone with an account can seal any file.

What it gives you is a dated, checkable record that this exact file was sealed under your account at that time. That can help when someone questions your work, but it isn't proof of who made it.

Who signs the seal: me or SecurityArts?

SecurityArts does. Every seal is signed by the SecurityArts service key, and the signer id on the certificate identifies that key, not you. You're identified by your account; you don't hold or manage a signing key. The public key is published at /api/seal/pubkey, so anyone can check a signature.

03 Checking a seal

Checking a seal

Can a seal be faked or stripped like metadata?

The signature covers the exact image bytes, so changing even one pixel breaks the match. And unlike embedded metadata such as C2PA, the certificate doesn't live inside the file where it can be stripped: it's hosted and can be checked on its own. The flip side: a re-saved, resized or screenshotted copy has different bytes and won't match. Keep your original file.

How is this different from C2PA / Content Credentials?

C2PA attaches provenance metadata to the file, which can be removed when the image is re-exported or uploaded somewhere that strips metadata. A SecurityArts seal is a signature over the bytes with a public certificate that lives outside the file, so a platform can't strip it. One limit applies to both: a screenshot or re-encoded copy has different bytes and won't match. The full comparison.

How do I check someone else's seal?

Open the work's certificate page, or drop the file into Verify. Anyone can check a seal without an account; checking is public by design.

Can I check a seal programmatically?

Yes. GET /api/verify/<sha256-hex> returns whether a sealed work with that fingerprint exists, whether its signature checks out against the SecurityArts public key, the signer id and the sealing time. The public key itself is at GET /api/seal/pubkey.

04 Using SecurityArts

Using SecurityArts

What files and sizes can I seal?

PNG, JPG and WebP images, up to about 6 MB. Identical bytes can only be sealed once: if a file has already been sealed, you'll be told so rather than given a second seal.

Is it free?

Sealing your work and checking any seal are free, and so is most of SecurityArts. Pro ($5 a month) and Pro+ ($15 a month) add extras for collectors and for artists who sell; paid plans open when payments go live. See what each plan includes.

Does sealing my work affect my copyright?

No. A seal is a dated record that your account sealed a specific file. It doesn't transfer, register or change any rights you already hold in your work.
