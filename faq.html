FAQ

Questions about the seal.

Plain answers about what a SecurityArts seal is, what it proves, and what it deliberately doesn't.

  • What is a SecurityArts seal?
    A seal is a cryptographic certificate for a piece of art. SecurityArts computes a SHA-256 fingerprint of the exact image bytes and signs it with an ECDSA P-256 key. The result is a public record — algorithm, hash, signature, signer, and timestamp — that anyone can verify.

  • Is SecurityArts an AI detector?
    No. AI detectors estimate a probability and are often wrong. A seal never guesses. It records that a specific account sealed a specific file at a specific time, and proves the bytes haven't changed since. It's a receipt, not a verdict.

  • What does a seal actually prove?
    It proves a specific file was sealed by a SecurityArts account at a recorded moment, and that the exact bytes haven't changed since. It doesn't prove originality or resolve copyright — it establishes a verifiable point in time you can point to.

  • Does a seal prove a human made the art?
    No. A seal proves that a specific account presented these exact bytes to SecurityArts at a recorded time, and that SecurityArts signed the SHA-256 of those bytes with its key. It can't see how the file was made, so it doesn't prove the work is human-made or original — anyone with an account can seal any file. What it gives you is a dated, checkable record that this exact file was sealed under your account at that moment, which is usually what an accusation turns on.

  • Who signs the seal — me or SecurityArts?
    SecurityArts does. Every seal is signed by the SecurityArts service key, and the signer id on the certificate identifies that key — not you. You're identified by your account; you don't hold or manage a signing key. The public key is published at GET /api/seal/pubkey so anyone can check a signature.

  • Can a seal be faked or stripped like metadata?
    The signature is verified against the exact image bytes, so altering even one pixel breaks it. Unlike embedded metadata such as C2PA, the proof doesn't live inside the file where it can be stripped — the certificate is hosted and checkable independently.

  • How is this different from C2PA / Content Credentials?
    C2PA attaches provenance metadata to the file, which can be removed when the image is re-exported or uploaded to platforms that strip metadata. A SecurityArts seal is a signature over the bytes with a public certificate that lives outside the file, so a platform can't strip it. One limit applies to both: a screenshot or re-encoded copy has different bytes and won't match the seal — the original file does.

  • How do I verify someone else's seal?
    Open the work's seal page or use the Verify tool. Anyone can check the signature against the image without an account — verification is public by design.

  • Can I verify a seal programmatically?
    Yes. GET /api/verify/<sha256-hex> returns whether a sealed work with that hash exists, whether its signature verifies against the SecurityArts public key, the signer id, and the sealing timestamp. The public key itself is at GET /api/seal/pubkey.

  • What files and sizes can I seal?
    Image files. Uploads are currently capped at about 8 MB of encoded data, which works out to image files of roughly 6 MB. Identical bytes can only be sealed once — if a file has already been sealed, you'll be told so rather than issued a second seal.

  • Is it free?
    Sealing your work and verifying any seal is free. Paid tiers are not public yet.

  • Does sealing my work affect my copyright?
    No. A seal is a timestamped record that your account sealed a specific file; it doesn't transfer, register, or change any rights you already hold in your work.
