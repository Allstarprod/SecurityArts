03 Method

How the engagement runs

  1. Scope to an outcome

    We agree the techniques and assets that matter most: a named goal, not a bundle of hours.

  2. Emulate and engineer

    Run the attacks, measure coverage, then build and tune the detections that were missing.

  3. Seal the report

    A signed, dated report: what you can detect now, and where the gaps still are. Sealed, so anyone you share it with can check it hasn't changed.

  4. Retest

    We run the emulation again. The retest gets its own sealed report, so you can show the coverage was checked, and when.

05 The seal

The report is the deliverable

We seal the report with the studio's SecurityArts account. Anyone you share it with can check that it's the exact file we sealed, and when we sealed it.

What the seal shows

  • Which account sealed the report: the studio's.
  • When it was sealed, as recorded by SecurityArts.
  • That the file hasn't changed since. Change one character and it stops matching.

What it can't show

  • That your systems are secure. It covers the report, not the systems it describes.
  • Anything outside the scope we agreed, or anything found after the seal date.
  • An audit opinion or a certification.
  • A copy that has been edited, re-saved, re-exported or screenshotted. Only the exact file matches.

06 Questions

Questions

Do you replace our SOC?

No. We make the SOC you have, or the tooling you run, measurably better: we engineer detections, hunt for what's slipping through, and leave runbooks your team owns.

What do we actually get?

Tuned detections mapped to MITRE ATT&CK, threat-hunt findings, incident-response runbooks, and a sealed report of coverage: what you can detect now, and where the gaps still are.

Can you test our existing detections?
