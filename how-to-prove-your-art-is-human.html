Seal a piece

01 Guide · Updated 16 October 2026

Told your art is AI? What you can show.

Someone leaves "obvious AI" under a piece you spent two days on. You know the truth. The hard part is showing a stranger who has already made up their mind. Here's what holds up, and what only feels like it does.

The short answer

Nothing can prove how a piece was made. Not process files, not detectors, not a seal. What you can show is what a dispute usually turns on: that you held the source files, that your account had a specific final file at a recorded time, and that it hasn't changed since.

Of the five common options below, a hosted seal covers the last two in a form a stranger can check, and a platform can't strip it. A screenshot or re-saved copy has different bytes and won't match, so keep the original file.

Why this is hard

The accusation spreads faster than any defense. By the time you reply, the comment has a dozen likes. Screenshots strip your context; a repost erases your caption. So the real question isn't "am I telling the truth". It's "what can a skeptic check for themselves in ten seconds".

Nobody can watch you make a decision. Whatever you show has to attach to something outside you: the files, the timeline, the record.

02 The options

Five options, ranked by how well they hold up

  • Good habit, weak under pressure

    Show your process

    Post the timelapse, the layered source file, the messy early versions.

    Where it helps: For people acting in good faith, a file with 80 layers is convincing.

    Where it falls short: A determined accuser will say you faked the layers, and most viewers never click through to check. It also does nothing once your work has been screenshotted and reposted away from your process.

  • Not evidence

    AI detectors

    Run the image through a tool that scores how machine-made it looks.

    Where it helps: A platform can triage a flood of unknown uploads.

    Where it falls short: The score is a guess, and detectors flag real human work all the time. Pointing to a detector that agrees with you invites the accuser to point to one that doesn't.

    A seal vs AI detectors

  • Strong, but strippable

    C2PA / Content Credentials

    An open standard that embeds a signed provenance record inside the file.

    Where it helps: It's backed by major vendors and carries a real editing history.

    Where it falls short: The record lives inside the file, and many platforms quietly strip metadata on upload. By the time your work is on the feed, the record can be gone.

    A seal vs C2PA

  • A statement, not evidence

    The Not By AI badge

    A label you add to say a person made this.

    Where it helps: It plants a flag, and it moved the conversation forward.

    Where it falls short: Anyone can put the same badge on anything, so there's nothing behind it to check.

    A seal vs the badge

  • What we'd reach for

    A seal

    Seal the finished file and get a public certificate anyone can check.

    Where it helps: It's built for a stranger's skepticism. The certificate shows which account sealed the exact file, and when, and anyone can check it without trusting your word or installing anything. Change a pixel and it stops matching. A repost can't strip it, because the certificate lives on the web, not in the file.

    Where it falls short: It can't show how the piece was made, or that you made it: anyone with an account can seal any file. A screenshot or re-saved copy has different bytes and won't match, so keep the original.

    How the seal works

What useful evidence has to do

Every option above is trying to clear three bars. A stranger has to be able to check it themselves. It has to be tied to your specific file, not a general label. And it has to still be checkable after the trip through a platform that strips metadata and captions.

Process files clear the first bar sometimes. C2PA clears the first two until the metadata is stripped. A hosted seal comes closest to all three, as long as you keep the original file. None of them clears a fourth bar: showing who made the piece.

How to seal a piece

The one habit worth building

Whatever you choose, do it before you're accused. The worst time to gather evidence is in the middle of an argument you're already losing. Keep your source files, and seal each finished piece as you post it. Then the record is already there, and you have something to point to in one reply.

04 Questions

Questions

Can you actually prove art is human-made?

No one can prove how a piece was made, and a seal doesn't either. What you can show is what a dispute often turns on: that you held the source files, and, with a seal, that your account had this exact final file at a recorded time and that it hasn't changed since. That's a checkable record, not proof that a person made the work.

Do AI detectors prove my work is human?

No. A detector guesses a probability from the pixels, and it's wrong often enough that plenty of human artists get flagged as AI. It can help a platform triage unknown images at scale, but it can't stand as evidence about one particular piece.

What's the best way to be ready for an AI accusation?

Have your records ready before you need them. Keep your layered source files, and seal each finished piece so there's a public certificate you can link the moment someone doubts you, instead of scrambling to gather evidence mid-argument.
