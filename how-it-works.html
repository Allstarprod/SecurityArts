How it works

A receipt,
not a verdict.

A SecurityArts seal is a cryptographic certificate for one specific image file: SecurityArts fingerprints the exact bytes with SHA-256, signs that fingerprint with its private key, and publishes a certificate anyone can check. AI detectors estimate a probability, and they get it wrong. A seal doesn't guess — it records who sealed which file, and when.

  1. 1

    You upload the piece

    Drop in the finished work you made by hand. We read the exact bytes of the file — nothing is altered, nothing is added on top of your image.

  2. 2

    We hash and sign it

    We compute a SHA-256 fingerprint of the raw image bytes and sign that fingerprint with the SecurityArts private key using ECDSA P-256. Change a single pixel and the fingerprint no longer matches — the seal is tamper-evident by design.

  3. 3

    You get a public certificate

    The result is a shareable seal page: the algorithm, the hash, the signature, the signer, and the timestamp. When someone claims your work is AI, you point them at the seal instead of arguing — anyone can verify it themselves.

✦ What does a seal prove?

  • A specific file was sealed by a SecurityArts account
  • At a specific, recorded moment in time
  • That the exact bytes haven't changed since — verifiable by anyone

What does a seal not claim?

  • It is not an AI detector and never guesses
  • It doesn't prove a human made the work, or that the work is original
  • It doesn't judge the art — only records who sealed what, and when

Technical details

For anyone who wants to check a seal without taking this page's word for it.

  • Hash. SHA-256 over the raw file bytes exactly as uploaded. Nothing is re-encoded first — which is why a screenshot or re-saved copy produces a different hash and won't match.
  • Signature. ECDSA on curve P-256 (secp256r1) with SHA-256, made by the SecurityArts service key.
  • Signer. The signer id on every certificate identifies the SecurityArts service key — not the artist. Artists are identified by their account and don't hold a signing key.
  • Public key. GET /api/seal/pubkey returns the current signer id and public key.
  • Verify by hash. GET /api/verify/<sha256-hex> returns whether a sealed work with that hash exists, whether its signature verifies, the signer id, and the sealing timestamp.
  • One file, one record. Identical bytes can't be sealed twice.

The exact verification procedure is published alongside the public key.
