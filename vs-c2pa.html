SecurityArts vs C2PA / Content Credentials

The certificate lives
outside the file.

C2PA (Content Credentials) is a strong open standard: it embeds a signed provenance manifest inside the file. The catch is practical — that manifest is often stripped when an image is re-exported or uploaded to a platform that removes metadata. Newer C2PA versions also define externally hosted manifests and soft bindings, though adoption varies. A SecurityArts seal is hosted outside the file, so there's nothing on upload to strip. The honest limit, for both: a screenshot or re-encoded copy has different bytes and won't match the seal — the original file does.

Fair take: these are complementary, not rivals. C2PA is excellent for carrying an editor-to-platform provenance chain, and it's an open standard worth supporting. A seal is the hosted receipt that still stands when a platform strips the metadata. Use both — they cover each other's gaps.
