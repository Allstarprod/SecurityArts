01 Compare · C2PA

The certificate lives outside the file.

C2PA (Content Credentials) is a strong open standard: it embeds a signed provenance manifest inside the file. The catch is practical: that manifest is often stripped when an image is re-exported or uploaded somewhere that removes metadata. A SecurityArts certificate lives outside the file, so there's nothing in it to strip.

02 Side by side

Two tools, two different jobs

Both sign something. They differ in where the record lives and what survives an upload.

A SecurityArts seal and C2PA / Content Credentials, point by point

03 The fair take

Complementary, not rivals

These are complementary, not rivals. C2PA is excellent for carrying an editing history from tool to platform, and it's an open standard worth supporting.

A seal is the hosted receipt that still stands when a platform strips the metadata. Neither one can show who made a piece: C2PA records what the tools reported, a seal records which account sealed the exact file, and when.

Use both if you can. They cover each other's gaps.

04 Questions

Questions

Is C2PA bad?

Not at all. C2PA / Content Credentials is a strong open standard for provenance, backed by major vendors. Its limit is practical: the embedded manifest is often stripped when an image is re-exported or uploaded somewhere that removes metadata. Newer versions add externally hosted manifests and soft bindings, but adoption varies.

Can I use C2PA and a SecurityArts seal together?

Yes, and it's a good combination. C2PA carries the editing history inside the file; a SecurityArts certificate is a hosted receipt a platform can't strip. They cover each other's gaps.
